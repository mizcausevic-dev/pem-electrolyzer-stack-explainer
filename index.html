    Five chapters, one causal chain

    Each camera move keeps the previous stage on screen, so you can see what causes what: water arrives, power splits it, gas leaves, heat follows, and the plant supports all of it.

    How to read the colors and shapes

    The three accents are explanatory overlays, not literal light or emission. They mark roles, so the same color means the same job at every scale.

    Cyan · primary pathway

    Water, protons, and product gas: the material that moves through the stack.

    Gold · energy and actuation

    DC power, electrons, heat, and actuators such as the circulation pump.

    Violet · sensing and state change

    Gas formation events, temperature states, sensors, and control signals.

    Solid fills · hardware

    Plates, layers, and equipment. Cut solids in the section view carry diagonal hatching.

    Translucent contours · fields

    The heat field in the cooling chapter: dashed isotherms and gradients, not objects.

    Grid panels · math and diagrams

    Equations, the charge-path schematic, and the qualitative temperature diagram.

    Accuracy and review status

    • Anode and cathode half-reactions and the net reaction (U.S. DOE).

    • Typical PEM operating range of about 70–90 °C, shown as a band, not a value (U.S. DOE).
    • Cells electrically in series, water and gas in parallel; end plates; layer materials described as “typical” (NREL).

    Illustrative by design

    • Generic PEM stack, not any product. Six cells shown; real stacks vary.
    • Not to scale. Layer thicknesses are exaggerated so each layer is legible.
    • Molecular scale is schematic and shown in slow motion; flow speeds are illustrative.
    • The heat field and temperature diagram are qualitative. No values are implied.
    • No efficiency, output, cost, or lifetime claims. No invented specifications.
    • Camera, palette, cutaways, and timing are original illustrative directions.

    How it is built

    Deterministic timeline

    The renderer is a pure function of the frame index. Camera keys, fades, bubble emitters, and molecular cycles derive from the frame number and a seeded hash. There is no wall clock and no Math.random, so frame 612 renders identically in the browser, in the video export, and on every replay.

    One world, many scales

    Stack, cell, catalyst interface, and plant share one coordinate system. Zoom interpolates in log space about a fixed point, so macro-to-micro dives do not swing sideways. Detail fades in by zoom level, and line widths stay constant on screen.

    Canvas 2D, TypeScript, Vite

    No animation library and no 3D engine. Headless Chrome renders each frame at 1920×1080 and ffmpeg encodes the 1,020 frames to H.264 at 30 fps. The same code produces the hero image.
